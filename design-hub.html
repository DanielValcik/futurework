Trezor brand & design hub

Structure and notes for unifying brand, identity, design system into one hub.

v1 / March 2026

Brand DNA

The most stable layer. Changes once a decade. Defines why Trezor exists, who it serves, and what makes it different. Everyone in the company should know this by heart.

Vision
Where we're heading. Uncompromising self-custody for everyone.
Mission
What we do daily to get closer to the vision. More measurable, helps prioritize.
Values
Transparency, open-source, security — at Trezor these are engineering decisions, not marketing slogans.
Audience & positioning
Who we serve, and why we're different. Shapes voice and product roadmap.
Design direction

How Trezor should feel as an experience. Not visual rules, but design philosophy — e.g. "security must never be complicated" or "transparency is a design material." Acts as a filter for every design decision.

Design vision
One sentence describing how people should feel using Trezor. Drives creative direction.
Design values
How the design team thinks — not what it creates.
Identity

How Trezor looks, speaks, and exists physically. Three dimensions of one brand under a single roof — because together they form the complete brand experience, even though each has a different maintainer.

Verbal

How Trezor speaks.
Voice & tone
Voice is constant (trustworthy, direct, precise). Tone adapts to context — onboarding = warm, security alert = urgent.
Terminology & glossary
Recovery seed vs. backup phrase vs. recovery share (Shamir). Definitive terms. Inconsistency in a security product = risk.
UX writing guidelines
Error messages, CTAs, onboarding copy, security messaging. Connects verbal identity to product patterns.
Security messaging
Trezor-specific — how we communicate security concepts, warnings, recovery processes. A bad formulation = user loses funds.

Physical

How Trezor exists in the real world. For most tech companies this is marginal — for Trezor, the physical product is a core brand touchpoint.
Industrial design language
Form, materials, surfaces, ergonomics. Anodized aluminum, Gorilla Glass, edge radii — brand decisions that define how Trezor feels in hand.
Packaging & unboxing
First physical impression. Must communicate security and premium quality. Tamper-evident elements are brand experience, not just security features.
Material & finish specs
How does Trezor green translate to anodization? How does box color correspond to the digital palette? Bridge between digital and physical.
Retail & spatial design
What a Trezor booth looks like at Bitcoin Conference. Must be recognizable as Trezor from 10 meters — visual identity applied to physical space.
Application

Where the system gets applied. Three domains with different needs but a shared foundation. Product draws from the design system, Marketing from Identity/Visual + Verbal, Hardware from Identity/Physical.

Product

Digital products where users spend most of their time. Draws directly from the design system — components, tokens, patterns.
Design system DS designer

Foundations

Base building blocks — tokens, grid, spacing. The bridge between brand identity and code.
Design tokens
Named values: color.brand.primary, space.md. Three tiers: primitive, semantic, component.
Grid & layout
Responsive grid, breakpoints, spacing system. Shared rhythm across Suite, web, landing pages.
Typography scale
Size scale linked to tokens. heading-1 through body-small with line-height and letter-spacing.

Components

UI library — buttons, inputs, cards. Exist in Figma and code. Documented with usage, states, a11y.
Core components
Button, input, checkbox, tooltip — the foundation used everywhere.
Composed components
Header, navigation, transaction card, device selector — built from core components.
Accessibility
WCAG AA minimum. Contrast and keyboard nav standards for every component.

Patterns

Repeatable UX patterns — how onboarding works, how transactions display. Behavior and flow.
Common flows
Device setup, send transaction, recovery — definitive steps for each flow.
Content patterns
Error messages, empty states, onboarding copy. Verbal identity in product.
Security patterns
How we display seed, confirm transactions, communicate risk. The most critical section.
Trezor Suite
Main app — desktop/web. Largest consumer of the design system. Components and patterns are primarily designed for Suite.
Trezor.io
Marketing site + e-commerce. Shares identity and base tokens with Suite but has its own layout patterns and conversion components.
Landing pages
Campaign pages. More flexible than the main site — can experiment more, but must stay within the identity.

Marketing & creative

Everything beyond the product. Draws from Identity/Visual and Identity/Verbal. Has its own templates and creative playbook.
Campaigns & social
Social media visuals, ads, PR. Need flexibility within brand identity. Creative playbook defines what's "on brand."
Creative playbook
How to use brand identity creatively — good/bad examples, moodboards, art direction for campaigns.
Merch & swag
Branded materials. Must respect visual identity but with more freedom than product design.
Booths & events
Physical installations at conferences and retail. Draws from Identity/Physical (spatial) and Identity/Visual (graphics).

Hardware & physical

Physical products and spaces. Draws from Identity/Physical for design language, materials, spatial design. Realizes what Identity defines.
Physical products
Safe 3, Safe 5, Safe 7 — each device realizes the industrial design language from Identity/Physical. Cross-line consistency is key.
Packaging production
Realizing packaging guidelines — materials, print, tamper-evident elements, inserts. Connects design intent with manufacturing.
Asset library

Central storage for all brand and design assets. Logos, icons, templates, photography, fonts — everything teams need to create on-brand work, organized and always up to date.

Governance & operations

Without governance, the system dies. Someone must decide what changes, how it's communicated, and how new people learn what to use. At 200 people, one person is enough — but they must be clearly assigned.

Versioning & changelog
When a button or color changes, how do 200 people find out? Changelog + notifications. Otherwise, stale versions everywhere.
Status indicators
Every page: stable / draft / deprecated / planned. People must know if a guideline is safe to use or still in flux.
Onboarding
New designer, developer, or external partner — how do they get into the hub? What do they read first? Role-based entry points.
